## What the animated winners do

- **Better Than Yesterday**: the clearest model for Coinflip Mind. Simple stick-figure "you" character, a problem
  everyone has, the brain science, then the fix. Thumbnails: one big object or face, 2–3 words ("DOPAMINE DETOX",
  "THE 20 SECOND RULE").
- **The Swedish Investor**: the only big animated money channel. A recurring cartoon character, famous investors and
  books, big numbers ("$1,000,000"), lists ("12 Mistakes"). Thumbnails: the character plus one bold number.
- **After Skool**: hand-drawn, emotional, idea-driven; proves rougher, artistic animation works.
- **The Infographics Show**: expressive cartoon faces reacting to a situation ("HAPPENING AGAIN", "WTF MICROSOFT???").
- **Improvement Pill / The School of Life**: huge subscriber counts but low typical views now. A warning: what built
  the channel isn't what's working today.

## The opportunity

Almost nobody does **animated money psychology**. The Swedish Investor covers investing and books; Better Than
Yesterday covers the brain and habits. Coinflip Mind sits between them: Better Than Yesterday's "your brain + the
fix" style, applied to money.

## The recipe to copy

1. One recurring simple character ("you").
2. A problem the viewer has, explained by their brain, then the fix.
3. Thumbnails: one face or object, an emotion, 2–3 big words or one number.
4. 10–15 minutes.

## Deep dive: Better Than Yesterday vs The Swedish Investor

(From each channel's last ~40 long videos, ranked against its own median, plus the top-8 thumbnails. Transcripts weren't
reachable from this session.)

**Better Than Yesterday:** hits are 8–78x its median: "How I Tricked My Brain…" (78x), "The 20 Second Rule" (19x),
"Why You Can't FOCUS - And How To Fix That" (15x). Flops (0.1x): "Why Everything You Do Matters", "Hard Work Is NOT
Enough", and a money video, "Making $100,000 is Hard…".
- Thumbnails: off-white background, hand-drawn doodle style, 2–4 giant words filling half the frame, one simple
  drawing (a head, a stopwatch, a desk). Same look every time.
- The thumbnail text is not the title: title "How I Tricked My Brain To Like Doing Hard Things", thumbnail "DOPAMINE
  DETOX".
- Every hit names a concept: "Dopamine Detox", "The 20 Second Rule", "Mini Habits", "1PM–4PM".
- 13–14 min.

**The Swedish Investor:** hits are 2–34x its median: "The Intelligent Investor's Road to $1,000,000" (34x), "Warren
Buffett: 12 Mistakes Every Investor Makes" (14x), "Atomic Habits" (4.6x). Flops (0.1–0.3x): monthly "Stock Market
News", personal portfolio updates.
- Thumbnails: branded teal/blue background, a bear logo top right, a famous real face (Buffett, Munger) or the cartoon
  character, one bold red/white phrase or number ("$1,000,000", "$100 PER WEEK", "NO ONE IS SAFE!").
- Borrowed authority: famous investors and famous books carry the click.
- 12–24 min.

**What both do**
1. One recognisable thumbnail system: same background, font and corner logo, so the channel is spotted in a feed.
2. Thumbnail and title split the job: the title says the topic, the thumbnail says 2–4 words or one number.
3. Each video sells one named idea ("The 20 Second Rule", "12 Mistakes", "$1,000,000").
4. Evergreen only. Their flops are news, updates, personal results, or vague titles.
5. A benefit for the viewer: a fix, a method or a mistake to avoid.

**How they differ:** BTY is drawn and self-help ("your brain"); SI uses real famous faces and numbers ("borrowed
authority").

**For Coinflip Mind:** BTY's drawn style and "your brain + fix" titles, plus SI's money anchors (famous books and people,
big real numbers). A fixed thumbnail system: one background, one bold font, a small coin logo in the corner, 2–4 words.
Name the idea in every video ("The $9.99 Trick", "The Pain of Paying"). No news or updates.

## Script breakdown (from the creator's transcript screenshots)

### The Swedish Investor: "The Intelligent Investor's Road to $1,000,000" (23 min), first 2 minutes
- 0:00 Hook = a character's result, then the gap: "At the age of 43, Tom retired… although, on the day he graduated
  college, he didn't have a penny to his name." The payoff comes first, then the contrast.
- 0:08–1:12 Relatable setup with concrete detail: a named job (GM), a supervisor with a golden watch who only talks about
  wild boar hunting, a father who can only hike once a month. It mirrors the viewer's fear (stuck in a 9-to-5).
- 1:21 Thesis in one line: "Ironically, making money seemed like it was costing them too much."
- 1:21 Roadmap: "This video will show you three stages…"
- 1:38 Stakes as a number: without investing, 57 instead of 43, "14 years of the most significant currency in life:
  time".
- 1:58 Open loops: "what he focused on… and, perhaps most importantly, what he didn't do".
- Chapters in the video ("Intro", …).

### Better Than Yesterday: "How I Tricked My Brain To Like Doing Hard Things" (14 min), first 3.5 minutes
- 0:07 Hook = calling out the viewer: "You probably don't have a problem playing video games… for 2 hours… But what
  about half an hour of studying? Oof." A contrast everyone recognizes, with light humor.
- 0:34 The tension: "Even though you logically know… you still prefer…"
- 0:48 States the obvious explanation, then moves past it: "One might argue it's obvious why…"
- 0:59–1:05 The real question: "Why are some people more motivated… And is there a way to make doing difficult things
  easy?"
- Named chapters ("Chapter 2: EASY HARD").
- 1:14 One concept, plus correcting a misconception: "Dopamine is often considered a pleasure molecule. But that's not
  quite what it does."
- 1:32–2:32 Science as a story: the rat lever experiments (rats refused to eat or sleep; blocking dopamine made them
  stop moving).
- 2:41 Bridges back to "you": "you can see similar effects… in our daily lives."
- 2:48–3:26 The mechanism in plain words, with an everyday example (comfort food), and a sting: "even if it actually
  makes you feel worse."

### What both scripts do
1. Within 10 s: a relatable moment with a contrast (no money at 23, retired at 43; games for 2 hours, can't study for 30 min).
2. Concrete specifics everywhere: ages, companies, durations, objects.
3. By 1:00–1:30: the question or promise, plus a roadmap ("three stages", chapters).
4. Stakes as a number ("14 years").
5. Open loops paid off later ("what he didn't do").
6. Evidence told as a story (an experiment, a character), then bridged back to "you".
7. One misconception corrected (an "aha" moment).
8. Short spoken sentences, small asides ("Oof", "Hmm").

### Coinflip Mind script template (10–14 min)
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | A "you" moment with a contrast ("You'd never pay $10 for this. But $9.99? Already in your cart.") |
| 0:20 | The obvious explanation, then why it's wrong |

| 1:15 | One concept + the misconception it corrects |
